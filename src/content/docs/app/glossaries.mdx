## Overview

Glossaries ensure consistent translation of specific terms, names, and phrases across your content. They're essential for brand consistency, technical terminology, and product names.

## Creating a Glossary

1. Navigate to **Glossaries** from the sidebar (under Translation).
2. Click **New Glossary** or the **+** button.
3. Enter a descriptive name for your glossary.
4. Select the languages this glossary will support (source and target languages).
5. Choose how to populate your glossary:
   - **Empty Glossary**: Start with a blank glossary.
   - **AI Extraction**: Paste text and extract terms from it. **More options** offers the same settings as [AI Extraction](#ai-extraction) in an existing glossary.

With AI Extraction, the language of your text is detected as you type. If you haven't selected that language, it is added to the glossary languages, with a note under the text. You can remove it again. When the detection is unsure, for example between Malay and Indonesian, you'll see the likely languages as buttons instead: click the right one to add it.

After you create the glossary, it opens and shows the extraction progress, then the terms to review.

## Adding Terms Manually

1. Open your glossary.
2. Click **Add Terms**.
3. Select the language pair you're adding terms for.
4. Enter the source term and its translation.
5. Optionally add a definition or context note.
6. Optionally choose a **Letter case** mode (see [Letter Case Matching](#letter-case-matching)). It applies to the language you're adding the term in; automatically translated entries start with **Auto**.
7. Click **Add Term**.
8. Repeat for additional terms.

## AI Extraction

Extract terms automatically from your existing content. Extraction itself is free; you're billed only for translating the terms you add.

1. Open your glossary and click **Extract terms** (or create a new glossary with **AI Extraction**).
2. Paste text that uses your terminology (up to 200,000 characters). Long texts are split into parts and processed in parallel.
3. Optionally open **More options**:
   - **Presets**: ready-made instructions for game & lore, software & UI, legal, medical, and marketing & brand content. A preset fills in the instructions, which you can edit.
   - **Instructions**: tell the AI what to extract, for example "Include every character, place and spell name." Your instructions take precedence over the default selection criteria.
   - **Depth**: **Key terms** (only the core terms a translator must keep consistent), **Balanced** (every term worth keeping consistent, the default) or **Exhaustive** (every candidate, including minor names and one-off mentions).
   - **Max terms**: keeps the terms that occur most often in the text.
   - **Include definitions**: turn off to get a term list only. It is faster and leaves room for more terms.
4. Click **Extract terms**. You can close the window while it runs and come back to review the terms.
5. Review the candidates. They're ranked by how often they occur in the text:
   - Edit a term or its definition in place.
   - Delete terms you don't want, one by one or by selecting several.
   - Add terms the extraction missed.

   Terms that are already in the glossary are left out.
6. Click **Add N terms**. The terms are translated into all languages of the glossary and added to it.

**Best practices for extraction**:
- Use representative content that includes your key terminology.
- Pick a preset close to your content, then adjust its instructions.
- If too few terms come back, try the **Exhaustive** depth or add instructions. If too many, use **Key terms** or set **Max terms**.

To extract terms through the API, see [Terms Extraction](/api/glossaries/terms-extraction/).

## Editing and Managing Terms

- **Edit a term**: Click on any term in the glossary to modify it.
- **Delete a term**: Remove individual terms as needed.
- **Bulk import**: Import terms via CSV for large glossaries.
- **Export**: Download your glossary as CSV for backup or migration.

## Letter Case Matching

Glossary QA (the [term counter](/api/glossaries/term-counter/)) checks whether a text or a translation uses your glossary terms. Capitalization matters for some terms and not for others: "IT" (information technology) isn't the pronoun "it", and "mW" (milliwatt) isn't "MW" (megawatt), but "Email" at the start of a sentence is still "email". The **Letter case** column tells glossary QA which is which.

The setting belongs to each language of a term, so English and German entries of the same term can use different modes. It only affects glossary QA; it doesn't change how translations are generated.

| Mode | How it matches | Use for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Auto** (default) | Uppercase letters must match; lowercase letters may be either case. If only the first letter is capitalized, a lowercase occurrence is flagged as ambiguous for review | Most terms | "IT" ≠ "it" · "term" = "Term" = "TERM" · "Apple" → "apple": ambiguous |
| **Capitals must match** | Uppercase letters must match; lowercase letters may be either case | Brand and product names | "Apple" ≠ "apple" · "iPhone" = "IPHONE" |
| **Exact** | Every letter must match exactly | Units, symbols, code | "mW" ≠ "MW" · "pH" ≠ "PH" |
| **Ignore case** | Any capitalization counts, never ambiguous | Ordinary words that can't be confused with other words | "email" = "Email" = "EMAIL" |

In every mode, accents must match, except in text written entirely in capitals.

To change the mode of a term:

1. Open your glossary and select the language tab.
2. Click the edit (pencil) button on the term's row. To change another language's entry, expand the row first and edit that language's line.
3. Choose a mode in the **Letter case** column.
4. Click the check mark to save.

Click the info icon next to the **Letter case** column header for a short description of each mode.

## Using Glossaries in Translation

### In the Playground

1. Open the Playground.
2. Select your source and target languages.
3. Choose your glossary from the glossary dropdown (only matching language pairs are shown).
4. Run your translation - glossary terms are automatically enforced.
5. If a term isn't applied correctly, update your glossary and re-run.

### In API Calls

Include the glossary ID in your API request to apply terms programmatically.

## Glossary Status

Glossaries have status indicators:
- **OK**: Ready to use
- **CREATING**: Being set up
- **UPDATING**: Changes in progress
- **DELETING**: Removal in progress
- **ERROR**: An issue occurred

Wait for status to be OK before using in translations.

## Tips

1. **Keep terms specific**: Generic terms may match unintentionally.
2. **Use base forms**: Add "car" rather than "cars" - the system handles inflections.
3. **Regular maintenance**: Review and update glossaries as your terminology evolves.
4. **Test your glossary**: Use the Playground to verify terms are applied correctly.
5. **Organize by domain**: Create separate glossaries for different products or domains.
6. **Version control**: Consider duplicating glossaries before major changes for rollback capability.
